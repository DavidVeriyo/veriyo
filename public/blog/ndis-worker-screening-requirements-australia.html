NDIS Worker Screening: What Registered Providers Must Know in 2025

October 2025  ·  9 min read  ·  By the Veriyo team

The NDIS Worker Screening Check is one of the most misunderstood compliance obligations in the disability sector. Providers are fined, workers are stood down, and audits fail — not because anyone intended to cut corners, but because the rules are genuinely complicated and the paperwork gets away from you fast. This guide cuts through the noise.

What is the NDIS Worker Screening Check?

The NDIS Worker Screening Check (WSC) is a national background check that determines whether a person is cleared to work with NDIS participants. It was introduced in February 2021, replacing the patchwork of state-based checks that existed before, and is now mandatory for workers in risk-assessed roles at registered NDIS providers across Australia.

The check is administered by each state and territory's worker screening unit, but the outcome — a clearance or an exclusion — is recognised nationally. A worker with a clearance issued in New South Wales can use it when working for a Queensland provider, without needing a separate check.

Key point

The NDIS Worker Screening Check is separate from a Working With Children Check (WWCC). If your NDIS workers also support children, they may need both. Neither replaces the other.

Who needs an NDIS Worker Screening Check?

Not every person who works at an NDIS registered provider needs a screening check — only those in risk-assessed roles. The NDIS Commission defines a risk-assessed role as one where the person:

  • Delivers NDIS supports or services directly to a participant
  • Is likely to have more than incidental contact with participants
  • Is a key personnel member (e.g. CEO, director, operations manager of a registered provider)
  • Manages or supervises workers who deliver direct supports

This means it's not just your support workers and support coordinators — it typically also captures your team leaders, coordinators, and senior management. Cleaners, admin staff, and tradespeople who attend your sites but have no more than incidental contact with participants generally do not need a check.

Common mistake

Many providers only screen direct support workers and miss the key personnel obligation. If your CEO, operations manager, or board members are involved in the delivery of NDIS supports, they need a clearance too. The NDIS Commission checks this during audits.

Unregistered providers

Unregistered providers are not required to obtain NDIS Worker Screening Checks under the national scheme. However, if they support participants who are plan-managed or self-managed, they may still be subject to state-based screening requirements. Check with your state's screening authority if you're unsure.

Can workers start before their clearance arrives?

Yes — but with strict conditions. Once a worker has submitted their application and received an application reference number, they may work in a risk-assessed role under supervision while the check is being processed. This is called working under an interim arrangement.

During an interim arrangement:

  • The worker must not be left alone with participants
  • A cleared worker must maintain line-of-sight supervision at all times
  • The provider must document the interim arrangement and the application reference number
  • The arrangement ends immediately if the check results in an exclusion
What auditors look for

The NDIS Commission specifically checks interim arrangements during audits. If you can't produce the application reference number, the supervision roster, and evidence that the worker was never unsupervised — you're non-compliant, even if the clearance eventually came through. Document everything.

Expiry, renewal, and the gap problem

NDIS Worker Screening Checks are valid for five years. Workers are responsible for renewing their own clearance, but providers are responsible for ensuring workers don't operate in risk-assessed roles with an expired clearance.

This is where most providers get into trouble. A clearance is issued to the individual worker, not to your organisation. When a worker changes jobs, or when their clearance lapses, your organisation remains liable if they continue working without a valid clearance.

The multi-provider complication

Workers can link their single clearance to multiple NDIS registered providers through the NDIS Worker Screening Database (NWSD). This is good for portability — but it means you cannot rely on a clearance certificate alone. The certificate only proves the worker held a clearance at the time it was issued. You need to verify their current clearance status directly through the NWSD or through your HR system every time you need assurance.

The gap problem

A worker's clearance expires on a Tuesday. They don't realise, you don't realise, and they work Wednesday, Thursday, Friday. You've now had an uncleared worker in a risk-assessed role for three days. If this is discovered during an audit or an incident investigation, the provider is liable for a breach of registration conditions — not the worker.

Best practice: renewal reminders

Build at least two renewal reminder touchpoints into your HR process:

  • 90 days before expiry — notify the worker and prompt them to apply
  • 30 days before expiry — escalate to their manager if still not renewed
  • On expiry date — suspend from risk-assessed duties until clearance confirmed

If you're managing more than 20 workers, doing this manually in a spreadsheet is where things start to fall through the cracks.

What happens when a worker receives an exclusion?

If a screening check results in an exclusion, the worker is prohibited from working in any NDIS risk-assessed role. The exclusion is issued by the state or territory screening unit and is recorded in the NWSD.

As the provider, you must:

  • Remove the worker from all risk-assessed roles immediately
  • Not transfer them to another role where they have more than incidental contact with participants
  • Not disclose the specific reason for the exclusion to other parties (the outcome is confidential)
  • Document the action taken and the date
Note

Workers who receive an exclusion can apply for an internal review, and in some states a merits review through the relevant tribunal. Until any review is finalised, the exclusion stands and the worker cannot be in a risk-assessed role.

Your NDIS screening audit checklist

The NDIS Commission's certification and verification audits will check your screening compliance. Here's what you need to have in order:

  • A current register of all workers in risk-assessed roles, including contractors and agency staff
  • Clearance status confirmed via the NWSD (not just a clearance certificate on file)
  • Expiry dates recorded and a renewal reminder process documented
  • Interim arrangement records for any worker who started before their clearance was issued (application reference number, supervision records)
  • Confirmation that key personnel (CEO, directors, operations managers) are cleared
  • Process for removing an excluded worker from risk-assessed duties immediately upon notification
  • Evidence that contractors and agency staff have been screened (you can't assume the labour hire company checked)
Best practice

Treat your NDIS screening register as a live document, not a one-time onboarding checklist. Clearances expire, workers change roles, and new starters join. The register should reflect your workforce's screening status right now — not what it looked like six months ago.

Contractors and agency staff

This is one of the most common compliance gaps in the NDIS sector. Many providers assume that if a worker comes from a labour hire or agency arrangement, the agency has handled the screening. This assumption can leave you non-compliant.

Under the NDIS Practice Standards, you — as the registered provider — are responsible for ensuring that anyone delivering supports to your participants holds a valid clearance, regardless of whether they're directly employed or engaged through a third party.

Best practice is to include a contractual requirement in your labour hire or agency agreement that all workers supplied must hold a current NDIS Worker Screening clearance, and to independently verify that clearance through the NWSD before the worker commences with your participants.

The bottom line

The NDIS Worker Screening Check is not a one-time box to tick at onboarding. It's an ongoing obligation that requires active management — tracking expiry dates, verifying current status, managing interim arrangements, and maintaining records that stand up to audit scrutiny.

For small providers with a stable workforce of ten people, a well-maintained spreadsheet and a disciplined calendar reminder process might be enough. For providers with 50+ workers, high turnover, or a mix of employees and contractors across multiple sites, manual tracking is where compliance failures happen.

The NDIS Commission doesn't distinguish between "we forgot" and "we didn't have a system." Both are non-compliance.
