What the 2026 NDIS Reform Act Means for Registered Providers

October 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  By the Veriyo team

The NDIS Amendment (Getting the NDIS Back on Track) Act received Royal Assent on 20 August 2026. For registered providers, three changes demand immediate attention: a new 7-year record retention obligation, mandatory registration for SIL providers, and an upgraded digital payment system designed to catch fraudulent claims. Here's a plain-English breakdown.

What changed, and when

The NDIS has been through several years of review. The 2026 Amendment Act is the most significant legislative change since the scheme launched, targeting three areas the Independent Review identified as systemic weaknesses: fraud and non-compliance, provider quality, and scheme sustainability.

Not all changes kicked in at once. Here's the timeline that matters for registered providers:

1 July 2026
Mandatory SIL registration begins
New providers delivering Supported Independent Living must be registered. Existing unregistered SIL providers had until 1 October 2026 to apply or cease operating.
20 August 2026
Amendment Act receives Royal Assent
7-year record retention obligation takes effect. Digital payment system rollout begins. New fraud and non-compliance provisions active.
1 October 2026
SIL deadline for existing unregistered providers
Unregistered providers still delivering SIL after this date are operating outside the scheme and subject to enforcement action.
January 2027 (proposed)
Differentiated pricing for registered vs unregistered
Under consultation — registered providers may be able to charge higher price limits than unregistered ones. Not yet confirmed.
July 2027 (planned)
Further high-risk supports require registration
Additional support categories beyond SIL are expected to require registration. Categories not yet confirmed.

The 7-year record retention rule

This is the change most providers weren't expecting — and it starts now. From 20 August 2026, registered NDIS providers must retain relevant records for seven years. Previously there was no uniform statutory retention period under the NDIS framework; many providers defaulted to five years or whatever their state employment laws required.

What records must be kept for 7 years?

  • Rosters and timesheets
  • Service delivery documentation and progress notes
  • Service agreements and any variations
  • Invoices and claims made to the NDIA
  • Staff screening and qualification records — including NDIS Worker Screening Checks, Working With Children Checks, and evidence of qualifications
  • Incident reports and complaints records
  • Participant consent records
Action required now

If your records retention policy says five years, update it to seven. Check that your document management system or HR platform is configured to retain records for the full period — some systems auto-delete after a set period by default. Screening records in particular need to be retained even after a worker leaves your organisation.

Why screening records specifically matter

The NDIS Commission has made clear that it can audit provider compliance retrospectively — including looking at historical periods to investigate incidents or complaints. If a participant incident occurred two years ago and your records for that period have been purged, you cannot demonstrate that the workers involved were appropriately screened and qualified at the time. That gap becomes a compliance failure in itself.

Mandatory SIL registration

Supported Independent Living has historically had a mix of registered and unregistered providers. That ended on 1 July 2026 for new entrants, and on 1 October 2026 for existing unregistered operators.

Registration requires meeting the NDIS Practice Standards, passing an independent audit (certification audit for higher-risk providers), and having key personnel undergo suitability assessments. New SIL-specific Practice Standards have also been introduced, with stronger requirements around:

  • Worker training and competency documentation
  • Shared accommodation safety and quality
  • How SIL audits are conducted and documented
  • Participant rights in shared living environments
If you missed the October deadline

Unregistered SIL providers still operating after 1 October 2026 are in breach of the scheme rules. The NDIS Commission can take enforcement action including banning orders and referral to the Australian Federal Police where fraud is suspected. The path forward is to cease SIL delivery immediately and apply for registration — or formally exit that support category.

The new digital payment system

Rolling out from August 2026, the upgraded NDIS payment system is designed to give the NDIA greater real-time visibility over claims — flagging patterns that suggest over-claiming, duplicate billing, or fraudulent invoices before payment is made rather than after.

For compliant providers this is largely invisible in day-to-day operations. But it does raise the bar on documentation quality:

  • Claims must be supported by contemporaneous service delivery records — retrospective documentation is harder to defend
  • Rosters and timesheets must align with claims (the system flags mismatches)
  • Agency and contractor claiming must be separately documented from direct employee claiming
What this means in practice

If your team fills in timesheets the day after (or the week after) shifts, and your claims go in before the timesheets are complete, you now have a documentation gap the payment system can flag. The fix is making contemporaneous records the standard, not the exception.

Differentiated pricing — what we know so far

One of the more significant proposed changes — still under consultation at time of writing — is differentiated pricing for registered versus unregistered providers. The proposal would allow registered providers to charge higher price limits than unregistered ones, recognising the additional compliance burden that registration carries.

The recommendation was for this to start on 1 January 2027, but the NDIA has not confirmed the timeline. Providers should not rely on this being in place by that date, but should monitor the NDIS Commission's website for updates.

If it does proceed, it creates a meaningful commercial incentive for currently unregistered providers to register — and strengthens the case for registered providers to maintain their registration in good standing rather than letting compliance obligations slip.

Your 2026 reform action checklist

  • Update your records retention policy to 7 years and confirm your systems are configured accordingly
  • Audit your screening records — are NDIS Worker Screening Check records retained for all current and recent former workers?
  • If you deliver SIL: confirm registration is active; if not yet registered, stop SIL delivery and apply immediately
  • Review your SIL documentation against the new SIL Practice Standards (worker training evidence, shared accommodation safety records)
  • Check that timesheets and service delivery notes are completed contemporaneously, not retrospectively
  • Confirm that agency and contractor claims are separately documented from direct employee claims
  • Monitor the NDIS Commission website for the confirmed date on differentiated pricing
  • If your key personnel haven't completed suitability assessments, ensure this is scheduled
