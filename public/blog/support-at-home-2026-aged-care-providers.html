Support at Home 2026: What Aged Care Providers Must Know

October 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  By the Veriyo team

The Support at Home program replaced the Home Care Package and Commonwealth Home Support Programme from 1 July 2025. Twelve months in, providers are navigating new pricing transparency rules, a revised Quality Standards audit framework, and strengthened workforce obligations under the Aged Care Act 2024. Here's what compliance managers need to action now.

What replaced what

If you're still thinking in terms of Home Care Packages (Level 1–4) or CHSP, the programs you're describing no longer exist. From 1 July 2025, all government-funded home aged care was consolidated into a single program: Support at Home.

Replaced (closed 30 June 2025)
Home Care Packages
Levels 1–4, administered by providers as package managers. All existing HCP recipients transitioned to Support at Home on 1 July 2025.
Replaced (closed 30 June 2025)
Commonwealth Home Support Programme
CHSP entry-level services. Consolidated into the Support at Home program's lower-needs tier.
Now in force (from 1 July 2025)
Support at Home
Eight classification levels (SaH-1 through SaH-8), managed by the ACAT-assessed budget model. Participants are no longer assigned to providers as package holders.
Unchanged
Residential aged care
Residential facilities remain under the aged care funding instrument and are not part of the Support at Home program, though the Aged Care Act 2024 applies to both.

For providers, the shift means that participants now hold their own budget and can direct services. The provider-as-package-manager role has gone. Providers are now service delivery organisations competing in an open market — which makes pricing, quality, and workforce compliance more visible than ever before.

Pricing transparency from January 2026

From 1 January 2026, home care providers must publish their Support at Home service prices on the My Aged Care Find a Provider tool. Prices must be kept current — changes must be published within 21 days of taking effect.

This is a compliance obligation, not a suggestion. ACQSC can take action against providers whose published prices are out of date or inaccurate, and participants (or their representatives) can use My Aged Care pricing information to dispute invoices.

What must be published

  • Unit price per service type (per hour for support services, per visit for clinical services)
  • Travel charges (flat fee or per-kilometre)
  • After-hours loadings and weekend/public holiday rates
  • Any case-management or co-ordination fee structure
  • Cancellation and no-show fee policy
Common gap

Many providers have their base hourly rate on My Aged Care but haven't published their after-hours loadings or travel charge structure. Participants are being billed for those — if the published price differs from what's invoiced, you have an immediate compliance issue. Review your My Aged Care listing now.

The revised Quality Standards and what ACQSC is auditing

The Aged Care Quality Standards were strengthened in 2024 under the new Aged Care Act and the first revised audits under the new framework began in early 2026. The eight standards remain, but the expectations under several have been tightened:

Standard Key change or emphasis in 2026
Standard 2 — Assessment and planning Care plans must be co-designed with the person and reviewed at intervals specified in the plan (not just annually). Evidence of co-design is now audited.
Standard 3 — Personal care and clinical care Registered Nurse oversight requirements strengthened. In-home providers with clinical service delivery must document RN involvement in care planning for clinical needs.
Standard 5 — Organisation's service environment Now applies more explicitly to home care settings — providers must demonstrate that in-home service delivery maintains person dignity and safety, with documented risk assessments for high-risk home environments.
Standard 7 — Human resources Worker screening evidence, qualification records, and training completion must be maintained and producible on audit. The Act's 7-year record retention obligation applies here.
Standard 8 — Organisational governance Governing body must demonstrate active oversight of quality — not just receiving reports. ACQSC will interview board or governing body members.

The new audit process

ACQSC shifted to a continuous monitoring model in 2026. Instead of purely scheduled re-registration audits, the Commission uses notification data (incidents, complaints, consumer feedback) to determine where to direct audit activity. A spike in complaints or a serious incident notification can trigger an unannounced audit.

What triggers an unannounced audit

Multiple complaints about the same worker or service type in a 90-day window; a serious injury incident where the root cause analysis is not submitted on time; a pattern of late care plan reviews across the client cohort; or a referral from a state health authority. If any of these apply to your organisation, assume you are on the Commission's radar.

Registered Nurse requirements for home care

Under the Aged Care Act 2024, home care providers delivering clinical services must have a Registered Nurse responsible for clinical governance. This is a new obligation for in-home providers — previously the RN requirement applied primarily to residential facilities.

The RN responsibility includes:

  • Approving care plans where clinical needs are identified
  • Supervising enrolled nurses and personal care workers delivering clinical tasks
  • Reviewing incident reports involving clinical concerns
  • Certifying that clinical training for care workers is up to date
Contractors and agency staff

If your RN is contracted rather than employed directly, the clinical governance obligation still sits with you as the registered provider. Your contract must document the RN's clinical governance responsibilities, and their involvement in care planning must be contemporaneously recorded. A generic contractor agreement that doesn't mention clinical governance won't satisfy an ACQSC auditor.

Workforce compliance under the new Act

Standard 7 is where most providers are caught out. The strengthened Human Resources standard now requires providers to demonstrate — not just assert — that their workers are appropriately screened, qualified, and trained for the tasks they're performing. Key requirements in 2026:

Police checks and screening

The Aged Care Act 2024 mandated that all aged care workers complete a nationally coordinated criminal history check (NCCHC) through the Australian Criminal Intelligence Commission (ACIC). Unlike the NDIS Worker Screening Check, the ACIC check is a point-in-time check and doesn't update automatically — which means you need to manage renewal.

The Act requires a new check if an existing check is more than three years old. For a workforce that joined across multiple years, this means you likely have a wave of renewal obligations right now — and another wave coming.

Medication management training

If personal care workers assist with medication administration, they must have completed medication management training appropriate to their role. This must be documented, and the provider must demonstrate that training currency is tracked — one-off completion three years ago doesn't satisfy the standard.

Manual handling and infection control

These two training categories are specifically called out in the Quality Standards guidance as evidence ACQSC looks for during audits. Completion records that can't be produced within minutes of an auditor arriving are treated as if the training didn't happen.

What good looks like

A provider that satisfies Standard 7 in 2026 can show an auditor — for any worker, within a few minutes — their screening check date and when it expires, which qualifications they hold and the evidence, which training modules they've completed and when, and whether any conditions apply to their work (e.g. supervision required for clinical tasks). Paper files or spread across multiple systems is no longer sufficient in an environment where unannounced audits happen.

Your Support at Home compliance checklist

  • Confirm your My Aged Care service listing is up to date — including after-hours loadings, travel charges, and cancellation fees
  • Set a 21-day calendar reminder process for any price changes, so My Aged Care is updated within the required timeframe
  • Review care plan records — are co-design conversations documented? Are review dates specified and being met?
  • Confirm your Registered Nurse clinical governance arrangement is in place and formally documented
  • If your RN is contracted, update their agreement to include clinical governance responsibilities
  • Run a screening check currency audit — identify all workers whose NCCHC is within 12 months of its 3-year renewal date
  • Pull training records for manual handling, infection control, and medication management — can you produce these in under 5 minutes?
  • Review your home environment risk assessment documentation for in-home services
  • Check that your governing body meeting minutes reflect active quality oversight, not just receipt of reports
  • Review your incident and complaint data for the last 90 days — any patterns that might draw ACQSC attention?
